Estimate, not a bill. Distances are metro-to-metro along the interstate corridors your trucks actually run, so the first and last few miles are approximate. Toll rates marked are per-mile averages; unmarked rates are verified against the authority's published class-5 schedule. Rates effective — toll authorities raise rates every January (Indiana twice a year), so re-check before billing a customer.

Directional tolls. The Delaware River bridges on I-80 / I-78 charge westbound only (into Pennsylvania), and the Hudson River crossings charge eastbound only (entering New York). This console bills both in either direction, so an eastbound run into New Jersey reads about $18 high, and a westbound run out of New York City reads about $113 high — that one is large, so check the direction before you quote a load leaving the city. Chicago Skyway is quoted at the peak rate; overnight (10pm–6am) it drops to $32.40. Connecticut shows zero tolls because it genuinely has none — the state removed them in 1985. Georgia, Arizona and New Mexico are the same: no truck tolls exist (Georgia bans trucks from its express lanes outright).

🛣️ Routing is Interstate / National Network only. Every route above is one a 53' van can legally run — the solver will not put you on a road whose STAA designation we have not confirmed, even when that road is cheaper. What this is not is a truck router: it does not know low clearances, posted bridge weights, hazmat tunnel bans, local truck-route ordinances or seasonal restrictions. It answers “what will this lane cost?”, not “which ramp do I take?” — keep using your routing software and the Low Clearance Playbook for the actual road.

About the mileage. The estimate sums metro to metro along the interstate corridors, then adds a local-access allowance at each end. It runs a few percent short of a real routed trip, so treat it as a planning figure. When you have the real mileage, type it into “Trip miles” — fuel, cost per mile and the day count all recalculate off your number, while the tolls stay driven by the roads crossed. Tolls never scale with mileage: they depend on which facilities you cross, not how far the shipper sits off the ramp.
