🔧 Fix an overweight axle

Sliding moves weight between axle groups — it never lowers the gross. Move a couple of holes, then re-weigh (each hole ≈ 250–400 lbs).

⚙️ Drive tandem too heavy

5th wheel → FORWARD tandems → FORWARD

5th wheel forward shifts weight onto the steer; sliding the trailer tandems forward shifts weight onto the trailer group. Use whichever has room.

🚛 Trailer tandem too heavy

tandems → BACK

Sliding the trailer tandems rearward moves weight forward onto the drive axles. Watch state bridge law — see the note below.

🛞️ Steer too heavy

5th wheel → BACK

Rare on a loaded van. Sliding the 5th wheel back moves weight off the steer and onto the drives. Usually caused by a very forward 5th-wheel setting.

📊 Over gross (80,000)

sliding won't help

Sliding only rebalances between axles. If the gross is over, you must remove or redistribute freight (or burn/pull fuel). Reweigh before rolling.

🌉 Bridge formula calculator

The Federal Bridge Formula caps how much weight a group of axles can carry based on how far apart they are — it stops short wheelbases from concentrating load on a bridge.

W = 500 × ( (L × N) / (N − 1) + 12N + 36 )

📍 State bridge law limits your slide. California (and a few others) cap kingpin-to-rear-axle at 40 ft (KP-40), so you can't slide the trailer tandems all the way back there. Check the state you're running before you slide.
⚖️ Always weigh, adjust, re-weigh. Guessing a slide gets you a fine. Hit a scale first — find the nearest CAT Scale ↗ — and re-weigh after each move.
