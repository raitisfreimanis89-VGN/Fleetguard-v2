Low Clearance Playbook

How to check a route for low bridges in FleetSpy before a load leaves the yard — and what to do when the calculator flags one.

Trailer height 13′–6″
Enter in calculator 14′–0″
Max weight 80,000 lb

A trailer that clears on paper can still hit a bridge. Posted clearances drift as roads get repaved, low-clearance listings go stale, and the shortest line between a pickup and a delivery often runs straight through a rail viaduct built long before anyone imagined a 13′6″ box trailer. This is the routine for checking a route in FleetSpy before it goes out to a driver, and the calls to make when the calculator flags something.

  1. 1

    Open Route Calculator

    From the main menu on the left, select Route Calculator — the entry just below Load Board. It replaces the dashboard with a full routing map. Every load gets run through it before dispatch, not only the ones that already look risky on a map.

  2. 2

    Enter the route

    Enter the origin and the destination. Before calculating anything, glance at the city, state, and ZIP that auto-fill — they don’t always agree with each other. A ZIP typed one digit off can still resolve to a real city, in the right-looking state, at an address you never meant to ship to — and the whole route calculates against it.

  3. 3

    Switch on Advanced Mode, enter real dimensions

    Click Advanced Mode to expand the routing panel, and set two fields:

    • Weight (lbs) — 80,000. The standard federal maximum on the Interstate system without an overweight permit. Enter it even on a lighter load — it keeps the calculator off any bridge or road carrying a posted weight limit, so the route holds regardless of what’s actually on the trailer that day.
    • Height (ft) — 14′–0″, not the trailer’s actual 13′–6″.

    Why 14′, not the actual 13′–6″?

    FleetSpy only flags or routes around a clearance at or below whatever height you type in. Enter the trailer’s exact number and there’s no margin left for a repaved road that quietly ate two inches of clearance, a bridge that hasn’t been re-measured since it was posted decades ago, or a foot of snow sitting on the roof. Entering 14′ puts every bridge posted between 13′6″ and 14′0″ in front of a dispatcher before a driver gets near it — instead of letting one that clears by four inches slide through unflagged.

  4. 4

    Calculate, then read the map

    Click Calculate Route. FleetSpy plots the path and drops markers along it — depending on the area, that’s truck stops, weigh stations, and low-clearance points sharing the same map. Click anything sitting directly on your route line to see what it actually is and what it’s posted at. Don’t assume every pin near the line is the same kind of marker.

  5. 5

    Decide: pass, verify, or reroute

    Every flagged point on the route falls into one of three calls.

    Go

    Nothing on the route is flagged at or below 14′–0″. Dispatch the route as calculated.

    Verify

    A marker reads between 13′6″ and 14′0″ — or the route threads an old downtown grid or a rail-adjacent industrial corridor: the parts of a city built before a 13′6″ trailer existed, where a posted clearance is most likely stale. Chicago’s old viaducts are the textbook case, but any city with a century-old rail yard has the same problem. Check the specific bridge — a state DOT low-clearance list or a call to the local terminal settles it in a few minutes on an unfamiliar lane. Reroute if a reasonable bypass exists; otherwise dispatch it confirmed-clear, with the bridge and its clearance written into the driver’s notes.

    No-go

    A flagged clearance at or below 13′6″, or a bridge you can’t verify, with no safe alternative on the map. Don’t dispatch this routing as-is. Force a reroute around the low segment, or escalate to your safety lead before the load goes out. A trailer stuck under a bridge costs a lot more than twenty extra minutes on a wider road.

  6. 6

    Adjust the route

    To route around a flagged bridge, drop a via-point on the roads you want the truck to take instead of the shortcut, or toggle Avoid Highways / Avoid Tollways / Avoid Ferries under Route Preferences if the flagged segment ties to one of those road types. Recalculate and check the new path the same way — a reroute can trade one low bridge for a different one on the way around.

  7. 7

    Brief the driver

    A route that’s clear in FleetSpy still has to reach the driver as more than a green line on a screen. Note the specific bridges you verified — location and posted clearance — directly in the load’s dispatch notes, not just “route checked.” A driver who leaves the yard trusting a phone GPS instead of the briefed route is how a cleared load turns into a bridge strike. Confirm they’ve seen the notes before they roll.

If the calculator resets mid-session

Editing an address field after you’ve already calculated — or a destination that clears itself — resets the panel: a red-outlined empty field, Total: 0 miles, Nothing found. Re-enter both endpoints and recalculate. Weight and Height stay set in Advanced Mode, but it’s worth a glance before trusting a route you recalculated partway through.

Before you release the load

  • Route Calculator → Advanced Mode
  • Weight 80,000 lb / Height 14′–0″ — never the bare 13′–6″
  • Calculate Route
  • Click every marker sitting on the route line
  • Old downtown or rail corridor on the path → verify posted clearance
  • 13′–6″ or under, or unverified → reroute or escalate. Do not dispatch.
  • Verified bridges written into the driver’s dispatch notes
