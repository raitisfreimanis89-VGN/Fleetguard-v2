🔗 How many chains — our bags

Our shop packs 2 chains in one bag. Chain laws are counted by drive wheels, not bags — so here is what each bag actually covers on a drive axle (the dual tires that do the pulling):

👜 1 bag · 2 chains
2 chains → one outer tire each side

Covers the two outer duals of a single drive axle. Enough for light, posted "traction advised" conditions — but not full Colorado compliance.

Minimum / light
👜👜 2 bags · 4 chains
4 chains → all four drive wheels, one axle

Chains all four wheels of a drive axle. This is what Colorado Level 1 & 2 require of a truck — and the safe default for any western mountain run.

✓ Colorado-legal
📦 Rule of thumb for our trucks: load at least 2 bags (4 chains) before any Colorado / western winter run, and keep a 3rd bag aboard as a spare or for the second drive axle. California can post conditions needing up to 8 chains (4 bags); Oregon wants 6 chains (3 bags) carried. When in doubt, carry more — you can't buy chains at 9,000 ft in a storm.

🚦 Where the chains go

Chained wheels — the drive axle does the pulling Runs bare unless a sign says otherwise
⚠️ Chains always go on the drive (powered) axle first. A "dual" wheel is two tires side-by-side — "four drive wheels" means chaining both tires on each side of one drive axle. That is 4 chains = 2 of our bags. Some states also want 1 set on the trailer when posted.

📅 When it's in effect

Most chain laws are sign- and condition-triggered — they switch on when weather hits and posted signs light up, not on a fixed calendar. But several western states set a season when you must carry chains regardless of weather:

📌 Bottom line: from roughly October through April, any truck routed through the western mountains should already have chains aboard. Colorado is the strictest — carry chains the whole Sep–May window even on a clear day.

🚦 Reading the sign — the two systems

🏔️ Colorado — Levels

Level 1Single-drive-axle trucks must chain all 4 drive wheels. Cables not allowed. Others need snow tires or chains.
Level 2Every commercial truck must chain 4 drive wheels — single and tandem alike. Buses chain 2.
SpecA legal chain = a loop each side of the tire joined by 9+ cross-links across the tread.

🌴 California — R1 / R2 / R3

R1Chains required on all vehicles except passenger cars / light trucks with snow tires.
R2Chains required except AWD/4WD with snow tires on all four wheels (and chains aboard). Trucks: chain up.
R3Chains on all vehicles, no exceptions. Rare — severe storms only.
