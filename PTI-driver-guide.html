FleetGuard · Pre-Trip Inspection

The fifteen minutes
before you roll

A pre-trip inspection is not paperwork. It is the one point in the day where you can find a problem while it is still cheap — before it is a roadside violation on your record, a load sitting on the shoulder, or a crash. This guide walks you through every screen in the app, and what each option actually means.

Takes 12–20 min done properly Required by 49 CFR 392.7 & 396.13 4 steps in the app Sign in with your own cell number
01 — The point of it

What a pre-trip is, and why it is not optional

Before you drive, federal law says you have to be satisfied the truck is in safe operating condition. That is 49 CFR 392.7, and it lists what you have to check: service brakes and trailer brake connections, parking brake, steering, lights and reflectors, tyres, horn, wipers, mirrors, coupling devices, wheels and rims, and emergency equipment.

49 CFR 396.13 adds that you must also review the last inspection report on that unit and sign it if defects were written up. If the shop says a defect was fixed, your signature is you agreeing it was fixed.

The app is how this company records all of that. It timestamps the walk-around, keeps your photos, and puts your signature on it. Done honestly, it is the strongest evidence you have that you did your job.

Reason 01

It catches what inspectors look for

Lamps out, tread depth, air leaks, hub oil and brake adjustment are the most common roadside violations in the country. Every one of them is visible on a proper walk-around.

Reason 02

It keeps the violation off your record

A roadside violation follows you as a driver and follows the company's safety score for two years. Finding the same defect in the yard costs a phone call.

Reason 03

It protects you after a crash

If something breaks on the road, the first question is whether it was findable before you left. A timestamped inspection with photos answers that. Nothing else does.

Reason 04

It is your leverage

A defect logged in the app is a fact on the record. Once it is in there, nobody can quietly ask you to run on it — it is dispatch's problem and the shop's problem, not just yours.

The one thing that will end your career

Marking a check Pass on something you did not look at is a false record. Under 49 CFR 390.35 it is a federal violation to make a false report — and the app records how long your walk-around took, so a four-minute inspection on an 18-tyre unit tells its own story.

If you are short on time, tell dispatch you are short on time. Never solve it by clicking faster.

02 — Before you open the app

Walk the truck in one direction, every time

The app asks the questions in its own order, but your feet should follow the same loop every single day. A fixed route is how you stop missing things when you are tired or in a hurry. Start at the driver's door, around the front, back along the passenger side, around the rear, and forward along the driver's side to where you started.

1
In the cab, before you step out

Air builds to cut-out, low-air warning works, ABS lamp goes out, no check-engine lamp. Wipers, horn, mirrors, seatbelt. Read the last report on this unit and sign off any defect the shop says it fixed.

The timer is running from Step 1

The app starts a walk-around clock the moment you leave the unit screen and it prints on your report. That is not there to rush you — it is there to prove you were out there. Do the walk with the phone in your pocket, then fill the app in. Do not stand at the door and tap through it.

03 — Signing in

Your own cell number. Every time.

Enter your number

Enter the 6-digit code

There is no password. You type your cell number, the app texts you a six-digit code, and you type it in. The code lasts five minutes. If it does not arrive, wait for the resend timer to run out and tap Resend — do not start over with a different number.

Use the number on your driver file — nobody else's

The number you sign in with is what links this inspection to your driver record. It is how the company knows who walked the truck.

Signing in on someone else's number puts your inspection on their record and makes the report false. That includes a dispatcher, a mechanic, a trainer, or another driver signing in "to help you out". If it is your truck and your trip, it is your number and your thumbs.

Common problems

What happensWhat to do
Code never arrivesWait out the resend timer and tap Resend. Check you have signal — the code comes by text, not through the app.
Code says expiredThey last five minutes. Request a new one. Do not reuse an old code from an earlier text.
Number is rejectedYour number on file is out of date. Call the office. Do not borrow a phone.
You changed your numberTell the office before your next shift, not at 5am in the yard.
Phone is dead or lostCall dispatch. There is a paper fallback. There is no version of this where you skip the inspection.

The app notes that your number is used to sign you in and link the report to your driver record, and is not stored with the inspection itself.

Step 1 of 4

Confirm your unit

Step 1 · unit numbers

The truck number is pre-filled from the dispatch link you tapped. That is a convenience, not a guarantee — read it against the number stencilled on the door before you continue. If you were reassigned to a different truck this morning, the link is still pointing at yesterday's.

Truck number
Required. Must match the unit you are physically standing next to. Type over the pre-filled value if it is wrong.
Trailer number
Marked optional by the app, but fill it in whenever you are pulling one. Half the checks in Step 2 and Step 3 are on the trailer, and without the number nobody knows which trailer they apply to.
Recent units
Shortcut chips for units you have inspected before. Faster than typing — but only tap one if it is genuinely the truck in front of you.
Example

You tap your dispatch link and it pre-fills Truck #1031. You are actually in #0992 because 1031 went to the shop overnight. Type 0992 over it, add the trailer number, and continue.

If you had left it, your inspection would sit on 1031's file — and 0992 would show no pre-trip at all for today.

Read this before you tap Start walk-around

The screen tells you a walk-around timer starts on the next screen and that the inspection is logged with your report. Tap it when you are ready to walk, not while you are still drinking coffee.

Step 2 of 4

Tyre tread check

0 / 18 — nothing rated yet

18 / 18 — all axles green

Every tyre on the unit gets two ratings and every axle needs at least one photo. The diagram at the top is your progress map — the dots go from grey to green, amber or red as you work down the axles.

What the dots mean

OK
Tread passed and pressure is good on that axle.
Low psi
Tread is fine but at least one tyre is soft. Still a defect — it needs air before you leave.
Tread fail
At least one tyre on that axle failed on tread or damage.
Unrated
You have not looked yet. You cannot submit with grey dots left.

The two ratings on each tyre

Tread
Pass — legal depth, even wear, no damage.  Fail — below the limit, uneven or cupped wear, cuts into the belt, bulges, exposed cord, or a plug in the sidewall.
Pressure
Good — at spec, checked with a gauge.  Low — soft, or you can hear a leak. A tyre run low destroys itself from the inside even after you air it up.

Legal tread depth

4/32"

Steer axle — A1

Minimum in any major groove

2/32"

All other axles

Drives A2/A3, trailer A4/A5

Per 49 CFR 393.75. Those are the failing points, not targets — a steer tyre at 5/32" will not survive the week. Measure in the groove with a gauge, not by eye and not with a penny.

Automatic fail, whatever the depth

Ply or belt showing through the tread or sidewall · tread or sidewall separating · a flat, or a leak you can hear · any cut deep enough to reach the belt · a bulge · a sidewall plug.

Photos — one per axle, minimum

The app will not let you past this step without a tyre photo on every axle. Shoot the tread face square-on in decent light so depth is visible. If you fail a tyre, photograph that specific tyre — that photo is what gets the shop moving and what proves the defect existed before you drove.

"Pass axle" — use it honestly

The Pass axle button sets a whole axle at once. It exists so eight good trailer tyres are not eight minutes of tapping. It is a time-saver for tyres you have already looked at — not a way to skip the drives because they looked fine from ten feet away.

Example — the inner dual

You are on A3 and the inside tyre on the right is noticeably softer than its partner. Tread is fine at 6/32".

Set that tyre: Tread — Pass, Pressure — Low. Photograph it. The A3 dot goes amber. Then air it up and tell dispatch what you found, because a soft inner dual usually means a slow leak that will be flat by tonight.

Step 3 of 4

Safety checks

Six checks, none rated

Marking a check Pass

Two failures, with severity and notes

The remaining checks

Six checks, each one Pass or Fail. Mark Fail and three more controls appear: severity, a photo, and a note. All three matter — severity tells dispatch whether the truck moves, the photo proves the condition, and the note tells the shop what to bring.

The six checks and what fails them

CheckFail it when
Air leakAudible hissing, slow build-up, governor not cutting out, damaged or chafed air hose, pressure dropping with the engine off.
Windshield damageCracks or chips — especially in the wiper sweep on your side — torn wiper blades, cracked or loose mirrors.
Hub leaksOil or grease at the wheel hub or seal, oil thrown onto the rim or tyre sidewall, hub cap sight glass empty.
Lights & reflectorsAny lamp out — head, tail, brake, turn, marker, hazards, plate lamp. Missing, cracked or filthy reflective tape.
ABS lightDash ABS lamp still lit after start-up, on the tractor or the trailer.
Check-engine lightAmber malfunction lamp lit, or any red stop-engine lamp.

Choosing the severity

This is the field drivers most often get wrong, and it is the one dispatch acts on. Severity is not how annoyed you are — it is whether the truck is safe and legal to move right now.

Minor

Legal to drive. Shop it when convenient.

  • Chip or small crack outside your wiper sweep
  • One marker or clearance lamp out
  • Light film at a hub, sight glass still reading full
  • Wiper blade streaking but clearing
Major

Call dispatch before you move. Shop it today.

  • Audible air leak or damaged air hose
  • Brake, tail or turn lamp out
  • Visible grease or oil on the hub or wheel
  • ABS lamp stays on after start-up
  • Crack spreading across your line of sight
Critical

Do not move the truck. Call dispatch and the shop now.

  • Air will not build, or low-air warning at idle
  • Oil running from a hub, or a hot wheel end
  • Both headlights or all brake lamps out
  • Red stop-engine lamp, or a flashing MIL
  • Anything you would not want your family behind
If you are torn between two levels, pick the higher one

Nobody has ever been disciplined here for calling something Major that turned out to be Minor. The reverse is how people get hurt. A second opinion costs one phone call.

Writing the note

One line. What it is, where it is, how bad. The shop reads this before they see the truck.

Good
"Air hose damaged and leaking at the tractor–trailer connection, driver side" · "Small crack, passenger side windshield, outside wiper sweep, not spreading" · "Grease at left rear drive hub, oil on inside of rim"
Useless
"Broken" · "See photo" · "Same as last time" · "Needs shop"
Worked example — the screen in the middle

Two failures on one inspection. The air hose is damaged and leaking, so it is Fail · Major with the note "air hose damages, leaking air" — the truck does not move until that hose is dealt with, because it is a brake system defect.

The windshield has a small crack, so it is Fail · Minor with the note "small crack" — logged, photographed, and the truck can run while the shop schedules the glass.

Two defects on the same report, two different answers. That is severity doing its job.

Failing something is not a black mark against you

A report full of passes every single day on a truck with 325,000 miles is not a sign of a careful driver. Finding things is the job. The drivers who write up defects are the ones we trust.

Step 4 of 4

Review, sign and submit

Step 4 · review and sign

The top of this screen is your receipt before you commit: how many photos are attached, how long the walk-around took, and when you started. Read those three numbers. If the walk-around says four minutes, you both know what that means.

Current mileage
Odometer as it reads right now. Not the number from yesterday's report. This is what maintenance schedules the truck's next service against.
GPS location
Tap Capture GPS location. It records where the inspection happened. It says "not captured yet" until you tap it — that gap on the report is worth avoiding.
Signature
Sign with your finger. Clear wipes it if it comes out badly. This is a legal signature — you are certifying the inspection is true.
Notes for dispatch
Optional field, but this is your direct line. Anything that affects the run goes here — see below.
Save draft
Holds it unfinished. Use it if you get pulled away mid-walk. A draft is not an inspection — nothing is on file until you submit.
Submit inspection
Files it. Dispatch can see it immediately and you get a text confirmation.

What belongs in the notes

  • Anything that affects whether you can run — "air hose needs replacing before this trailer moves"
  • Things you watched, that are not yet defects — "left steer wearing on the inside edge, watch it"
  • Things the shop needs to know — "same hub as last week's write-up, still weeping"
  • Anything you fixed yourself — "aired up right inner on A3, was at 70 psi"
If you failed something Major or Critical — call, do not just submit

Submitting puts it on the record. It does not put it in anyone's ear. Pick up the phone. The report is the paperwork; the call is what stops a load being planned around a truck that is not going anywhere.

04 — After you submit

What happens to your report

Your confirmation and reference

You get a reference number, a summary of what you flagged, and a confirmation text. Keep the text. If anyone ever asks whether that truck was inspected before it left, that reference is the answer, and it is on your phone rather than in someone else's system.

Check the summary before you walk away

LineWhat to look for
ReferenceScreenshot it or keep the text. This is how the report is found later.
Tyres flaggedShould match what you actually found. If you failed a tyre and this says 0, something did not save — redo it.
Checks failedSame. Your memory of the walk and this number should agree.
PhotosIf this is lower than you expect, a photo failed to attach. Fix it now, not after the load.
DurationYour walk-around time. This is on the permanent record.
Where it goes

Dispatch sees it immediately and cannot plan your day around a truck you have flagged. Maintenance gets the defects with your photos and notes. Safety keeps it as the record that this unit was inspected, by you, at that time and place — which is exactly what gets asked for after a roadside stop or a crash.

The end of the day

The pre-trip is only half of it. At the end of your driving day, if you found a defect, it has to be reported on that unit under 49 CFR 396.11. If you fixed something or something broke during the run, it goes in the same way — do not carry it in your head to tomorrow.

Four things that are never acceptable

  • Signing in on a number that is not yours, or letting anyone else sign in as you.
  • Marking Pass on something you did not physically look at. That is a false federal record.
  • Filling the app in from the driver's seat. The timer and the photos both give it away.
  • Driving on a Critical defect because someone told you the load is hot. If you are ever told that, the answer is no, and safety wants to hear about it the same day.
05 — For dispatch

Reading a driver's inspection

Every submitted inspection lands with you before the driver rolls. Three things to do with it:

Do this

Open it before you plan the day

A Major or Critical flag means that truck's availability has changed. Finding that out at 6am is a schedule problem. Finding out at noon is a load sitting on the shoulder.

Do this

Watch the walk-around time

Four minutes on an 18-tyre unit is not an inspection. That is a coaching conversation the same day — quietly, and before it becomes a violation.

Never do this

Ask a driver to re-do a report

Asking a driver to soften a severity or resubmit without a defect is asking him to falsify a federal record, and it is coercion under 49 CFR 390.6. If a defect makes a load impossible, that is safety's call, not a conversation with the driver.

Never do this

Sign in on the driver's behalf

Not to save him time, not because he is running late, not for any reason. The inspection has to be done by the person who walked the truck.
